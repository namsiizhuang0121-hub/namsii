How Is Petit Planet Different From Stardew Valley? My Beta Take

How is Petit Planet different from Stardew Valley? Stardew is built around an inherited farm and life in Pelican Town. Petit Planet gives you your own small planet to tend and decorate, with the Starsea beyond it to explore.

Having played the Petit Planet beta, I liked that change of setting a lot. The farming connection is there, but the decorating and that romantic, starry look are what made me want to spend time with it.

Animal Crossing: New Horizons belongs in the comparison too, particularly for the home and characters. I'll compare those parts, rather than assume every Stardew player wants the same thing. Petit Planet's global open beta starts December 4, 2026, so this is an upcoming recommendation.

If you're looking for games similar to Stardew Valley, Petit Planet is one I'd consider for farming, decorating and exploration. That's the overlap I enjoyed in the beta.

Stardew also means different things to different people. Someone choosing it for romance or farm optimization may have different priorities from someone who mostly wants to fish and make their home look nice. A familiar activity doesn't tell you that another game's entire set of systems will match.

With Petit Planet, the strongest case I can make is for the parts I liked myself.

Stardew's inherited farm gives your home a place in a wider town. Animal Crossing: New Horizons puts the island itself at the center of making a personal paradise. Petit Planet makes your home a little planet you can build and decorate.

That was a big part of the appeal for me. Decorating my planet and home felt approachable in the beta. I enjoyed shaping somewhere that felt like mine, and the planetary setting gave it a different mood from a farm or an island.

For a decorating-first player coming from Animal Crossing, this is where I'd start. I'd look at the tools in use as well as the finished spaces. A lovely room can show what someone made; the process shows whether you'd enjoy making your own. My positive impression is that the decorating felt friendly to get into, rather than that I've tested every tool against every other life sim.

Farming is central to Stardew's premise. In Petit Planet, it's one of several things to do around your home: farming, fishing, beachcombing and cooking were all activities I enjoyed in the beta. I liked the little surprises as I went about things, too.

That matters if the farming in Stardew is part of a wider appeal for you. Growing things, fishing and cooking can make a place feel like somewhere you live. Petit Planet gave me that feeling alongside decorating my home.

For an Animal Crossing player, the familiar connection is fishing, gathering and having a personal place to look after. Petit Planet puts those alongside farming and cooking in its galactic setting. The question I'd ask is how much you enjoy those activities together. I'm recommending that mix, rather than claiming the farming has the same complexity or progression as Stardew.

The Starsea is the clearest difference I'd point to. Your planet is home; the Starsea is the exploration region beyond it, with destinations called Islets.

I enjoyed traveling outside my planet, gathering materials and coming across unexpected encounters. The setting had something to do with what I was enjoying in the game. I liked having a place to work on and other places to discover.

Stardew has a farm-and-town setting, and Animal Crossing: New Horizons has its island setting. Both have places beyond your immediate home, so exploration by itself isn't the difference. In Petit Planet, those journeys happen in this galactic world, which is a big part of its appeal to me.

If that idea catches your attention, include Starsea footage in what you watch. It explains more about Petit Planet's identity than decorating footage on its own.

Stardew has the people around Pelican Town. Animal Crossing has its animal residents. In Petit Planet, the characters you get to know are your Neighbors.

Their different personalities brought warmth and joy to my planet. I liked having that alongside the decorating: the place mattered, but so did the characters around it.

There's also the Galactic Bazaar, where players can meet friends. That's worth separating from the Neighbors. One is about the characters you befriend; the other is about spending time with other players.

So if you're looking for farming and social features, Petit Planet has reasons to interest you on both sides. The Neighbors were a positive part of my beta experience. For a specific activity with a friend, though, check that activity directly. The fact that a life sim has a social side doesn't tell you whether it supports the particular multiplayer setup you're looking for.

The art is where personal taste really comes into this comparison. Stardew's pixel art, Animal Crossing's island presentation and Petit Planet's starry world have very different appeals.

I preferred Petit Planet's look. It had a romantic feeling that I really liked, and I enjoyed spending time in that setting. If you watch the footage and feel the same, that's a perfectly good reason to be interested. For a game about making a home, liking the place you're looking at matters.

The beta's mechanics felt friendly and relaxing to me too. I felt I'd enjoy coming back regularly. That impression helps explain why I'd recommend it, but it doesn't establish that Petit Planet is less demanding than either of the other games in every respect.

There is stamina. I also can't judge final long-term demands or spending from these beta impressions. My recommendation comes from enjoying the activities and finding the experience welcoming.

For a Stardew player, I'd recommend watching Petit Planet if farming, decorating and exploring are your main interests. For an Animal Crossing player, I'd start with the home and Neighbors, then see whether the Starsea setting appeals to you.

The decorating and the look were the strongest draws for me. That's where my recommendation comes from. If your must-have feature is a particular romance system, a precise farming setup or a specific way to play with friends, check that before deciding.

Petit Planet deserves a look because of what it brings to these familiar interests. You can enjoy the overlap without expecting your entire Stardew or Animal Crossing experience to transfer across.
