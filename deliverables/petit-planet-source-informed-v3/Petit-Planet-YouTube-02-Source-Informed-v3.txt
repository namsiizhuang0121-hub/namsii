What Games Let You Customize Your Home or Environment? My Petit Planet Beta Notes

I liked decorating in Petit Planet's beta enough that it's the part I want to start with. You get your own little planet, and making that place feel like home was a highlight.

If you're looking for a game where you can customize your home or environment, I'd keep this one on your list. The global open beta starts December 4, 2026, so these are my impressions ahead of that.

It's HoYoverse's cozy galactic life sim, with farming, fishing, cooking and exploring alongside the building and decorating. I'll talk about those too, because they were part of what I enjoyed. But let's start with the place itself. This is the bit of the game that made me want to share my footage.

Working on my planet and decorating my home felt approachable. I enjoyed making the place my own, without feeling like I had to be an amazing decorator to get into it.

That's the part I'd look at closely in the footage if home customization is your main interest. Watch the process as well as the finished space. A pretty room can catch your eye, but you'll probably want to see how making it actually works.

I can say that I liked doing it in this beta. I wouldn't turn that into a claim that the game has every building feature someone might want, though. If you have a particular tool or layout in mind, check for that specifically.

What appealed to me was fairly straightforward: I had a little planet of my own, and I enjoyed shaping it and decorating my home. The setting helped a lot with that.

The starry look really worked for me. There was a romantic feeling to it that made the idea of having a home on this little planet especially appealing.

I personally preferred Petit Planet's look to Animal Crossing and Stardew Valley. Very much a taste thing. Stardew's pixel art has a completely different appeal, and loving one style doesn't make another style bad. This was just the one I was more drawn to.

When a game has a lot of decorating, liking the look of the world is a pretty big deal. You're spending time on a place because you want to enjoy being there. Here, the atmosphere was a reason I wanted to work on my planet in the first place.

Have a look at these shots. You'll probably know quite quickly whether the starry setting does something for you too.

I also enjoyed the everyday activities: farming, fishing, beachcombing and cooking. Beachcombing deserves a mention because it was part of the appeal alongside the more familiar farming and fishing.

I liked the little surprises as I went about those activities. That's harder to communicate through a list of features, but it was part of why spending time on the planet felt nice.

This is where I'd separate what I'm recommending from a pure building game. There are ordinary life-sim activities around the decorating, and I enjoyed those activities themselves. They weren't just something I wanted to skip past to get back to my home.

If you want a life sim where you can make a place your own and also farm, fish and cook, that's the combination I'd point to in Petit Planet. It made sense for the way I wanted to play.

The Starsea was another part I liked. You can head beyond your own planet to explore, with destinations called Islets. I enjoyed gathering materials and coming across unexpected encounters out there.

It gave the space setting more to it than a look. I had my own place, but there were also places beyond it that I wanted to explore. That was a nice change of focus from working on my home.

And it's a reason I'd mention Petit Planet to someone who likes decorating but also wants some exploration. Those were both things I enjoyed in the beta, rather than just liking one and putting up with the other.

I wouldn't want a video about the home to leave that out. Exploring the Starsea was part of what made this particular life sim interesting to me.

The Neighbors brought a lot of warmth to the planet too. They're the characters you get to know, and I liked their distinct personalities.

For a video focused on decorating, that might sound like a separate topic. But it mattered to how I felt about the place. I enjoyed having those characters around, alongside the everyday activities.

There's also the Galactic Bazaar, where you can meet other players. The Neighbors and the people at the Bazaar are two different sides of the social experience, and I liked both being part of the setting.

What stood out to me was that sense of warmth. It helped make the planet somewhere I enjoyed being, beyond how I could decorate it.

I found the beta friendly and relaxing, and it felt like something I'd enjoy coming back to regularly. There are still a couple of practical things I'd check before recommending it for a particular person.

It has stamina, for one. If having no stamina system is important to your idea of a relaxing game, you should know that.

I'd also look again at the final purchase options. Petit Planet is planned as free-to-play with optional in-app purchases, and the final items and prices matter, especially when decorating is a big part of the appeal.

Those are things I'd want to know alongside the footage. I enjoyed this beta, but that doesn't tell us everything about playing long-term. The decorating, activities and atmosphere are what this recommendation is based on.

So, if you're looking for home and environment customization inside a cozy life sim, I'd recommend taking a look at Petit Planet. Decorating my own planet was a highlight, and I liked the starry atmosphere around it.

Farming, fishing, beachcombing, cooking, exploring the Starsea and getting to know the Neighbors were part of the enjoyment too. That's why I'd point to it for someone who wants a home to work on inside a broader life sim.

The global open beta starts December 4, 2026, on PC and mobile. A Nintendo Switch 2 version is planned for 2027.
