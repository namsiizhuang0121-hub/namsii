If I Like Farming and Social Features, What Games Should I Try? Petit Planet Beta Review

If you like farming and social features, I’d put Petit Planet on your December list. I played the beta, and the combination really worked for me: farming and fishing, a little planet to decorate, characters I enjoyed meeting, and somewhere beyond home to explore.

Petit Planet is a cozy galactic life sim developed and published by HoYoverse. The global open beta starts December 4, 2026, so this is a recommendation for what’s coming up.

The bit I want to get into is the social side, because that word covers a lot. Getting attached to characters is one thing. Meeting other players is another. Petit Planet has Neighbors you can befriend and the Galactic Bazaar for meeting friends, and those do different jobs in the experience.

Here’s how they fit alongside the farming, and what I’d still want to check before making this my everyday game.

The everyday activities were a big part of why I liked the beta. Farming, fishing, beachcombing, cooking — that’s a pretty convincing collection of things to put in front of me. I enjoyed the little surprises along the way, too.

What matters to me in a farming life sim is what else I’m interested in around the farming. Here, decorating, getting to know the Neighbors and exploring the Starsea all appealed to me. The farming was part of spending time in a place I liked.

If growing crops is your main interest, look closely at that part of the footage. My recommendation comes from enjoying several activities together. Fishing and cooking were things I wanted to do as well, and decorating was a big part of the appeal. If that sounds like how you play farming games, Petit Planet is worth looking at. I found the mechanics friendly to get into, which helped the whole thing feel relaxing.

Decorating my own planet was one of my favorite parts. Shaping the place and decorating my home felt approachable, and I enjoyed having somewhere to make my own.

That’s an easy point of connection for someone coming from Animal Crossing. Petit Planet puts those familiar decorating interests in a galactic setting, and the starry atmosphere really suited my taste. There’s something romantic about looking after your own little planet.

Personally, I preferred that look to Animal Crossing and Stardew Valley. That’s a taste thing, but enjoying the setting matters a lot to me in a life sim. If decorating is one of the things you want around the farming, this was a strong part of the beta for me.

The Neighbors brought a lot of warmth to it. These are the characters you meet and befriend, and I liked their different personalities.

That’s an important distinction when someone asks for farming and social features. They might mean getting to know characters, or they might be looking for other people to play with. For the first of those, the Neighbors are the part of Petit Planet I’d point them toward.

I enjoyed having characters to care about alongside the everyday activities. That made the planet feel more welcoming to me. If character friendships are a big reason you enjoy life sims, take a look at the actual interactions in the footage. That will give you a better sense of the personalities than a quick list of features.

What I can recommend from my beta experience is the warmth they added. I wouldn’t turn that into a promise of every relationship system you might want, but I did like getting to know them.

The Starsea was another part I really liked. You travel beyond your home planet to explore places called Islets, gather materials and come across unexpected encounters.

I enjoyed having somewhere else to discover alongside the planet I was decorating. Those are two interests that work well together for me in a life sim: making a home, and seeing what’s beyond it. The galactic setting gave that exploration a feel I really liked.

For a farming player, this is another reason to look at Petit Planet. There’s more to spend time with around your home activities. I liked the exploration itself, especially the unfamiliar places and little surprises. Watch that part of the gameplay if exploration is important to you — it was one of the things that made me interested in coming back.

Then there’s meeting other players at the Galactic Bazaar. I enjoyed gathering with other Planet Tenders there, and it added a different kind of social experience alongside the Neighbors.

This is the part to look at if “social features” means other people to you. The Neighbors are game characters. The Galactic Bazaar is where players meet friends. Both appealed to me, for different reasons: I liked the characters’ personalities, and I liked having somewhere to gather with other players.

One thing I’d be precise about is what you want to do together. If you’re choosing a game specifically to run one shared farm or build the same house with someone, you’ll want confirmation of those exact activities. Meeting friends at the Bazaar doesn’t establish that.

My positive impression here comes from the gathering itself. I liked having that in the game alongside the farming, decorating and character friendships. It’s why I’d include the Bazaar when explaining Petit Planet’s social appeal.

There are still things I want to keep an eye on. Petit Planet has stamina, so finding it relaxing in the beta doesn’t mean there are no limits on what you do. I found the mechanics approachable for the way I like to play. Someone who wants to push every system as far as possible may care about different things.

It’s also planned as free-to-play with optional in-app purchases. Final items, rates and prices need to be judged from the game at official launch. I’d want to look at those details before making a long-term spending recommendation, especially for anyone who cares a lot about decorating and collecting.

Those questions matter, even though I enjoyed the beta. I can talk about the activities and characters I liked, and why they made me interested in playing more. Progression over a longer period and the final purchase details deserve another look when that information is available.

So, if you like farming and social features, who would I recommend Petit Planet to? Someone who wants farming as part of a cozy life sim, enjoys getting to know characters, and likes decorating and exploring as well. That’s the combination I enjoyed.

The Neighbors and the Galactic Bazaar are two different parts of that social appeal. Add the Starsea outside your home planet, and those are the main reasons I’d put this one on your list.
