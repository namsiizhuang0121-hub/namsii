If I Like Farming and Social Features, What Games Should I Try? Petit Planet Beta Review

If you like farming and social features, Petit Planet is one I would put on your December watchlist. I played the beta, and the strongest part for me was the combination: everyday activities, a little planet to make my own, and reasons to head out and meet characters and other players.

Petit Planet is HoYoverse's cozy galactic life sim. Its global open beta begins on December 4, 2026. The announced PC and mobile channels are HoYoPlay, the Epic Games Store, the App Store on iOS, and Google Play on Android. A Nintendo Switch 2 version is planned for 2027.

For this review, I’m looking at three things: whether the everyday activities appealed to me, whether the planet felt personal, and what the social side added. These are impressions from the beta I played, with the current release information kept separate.

The place you look after in Petit Planet is your own small planet. Farming, building and decorating all belong to that planet-life setup, and making the place feel like home was one of my favorite parts of the beta.

Shaping and decorating my planet felt effortless to me. I liked being able to put my attention into a place that was mine, rather than thinking about it only as a backdrop for the next activity. That personal connection is a big part of what I look for in a life sim.

For farming-and-social players, this is a real reason to consider Petit Planet: the home you work on has a clear identity. You are tending your own planet, and building and decorating are core activities. My positive impression comes from doing that in the beta. It does not tell you that every possible building tool is available, but the overall feeling of making a home really worked for me.

I also enjoyed the everyday mix of farming, fishing, beachcombing and cooking. Those were the activities that made planet life appealing to me, and finding little surprises along the way added to that feeling.

It is worth being specific about that. I’m not saying that every system is the deepest version of its genre, or that a beta proves years of replay value. What I liked was having those familiar life-sim interests together in this setting. The activities gave me different things to look forward to as I spent time on my planet.

That is why I would consider Petit Planet for someone who likes farming but wants a broader cozy life sim around it. The farming sits alongside cooking, decorating, fishing, gathering and beachcombing. You can judge the actual actions from the beta footage, while my takeaway is about the combination: these were enjoyable parts of the same planet-life experience, with small discoveries helping it feel warm and inviting.

The social side has two parts that are easy to mix up. Neighbors are the characters you get to know and befriend. Playing with friends is also a core activity, and the Galactic Bazaar is where players meet friends.

Meeting the Neighbors was a warm, joyful part of my beta experience. Their distinct personalities gave me another reason to care about the world around my planet. If character friendships are part of why you enjoy life sims, that is an important piece of Petit Planet’s appeal.

I also enjoyed gathering with other Planet Tenders at the Galactic Bazaar. That brought other players into the experience alongside the characters. For me, those two kinds of connection made the social side worth paying attention to.

I would still keep the description precise. This is not a claim that every activity supports shared play. Meeting friends does not, by itself, confirm a shared farm, shared building tools or cross-platform play. The recommendation is based on the character encounters and player gatherings I actually enjoyed.

Then there is the Starsea. Your own planet is the place you tend, build and decorate. The Starsea is the region beyond it that you travel out to explore, and its exploration destinations are called Islets.

Heading beyond my planet was another part I really liked in the beta. Exploring unfamiliar places, gathering materials and running into unexpected encounters gave the journey its own appeal. It made the setting feel like more than the home I was working on.

The starry look also really suited my taste. It had a romantic feeling that I found beautiful, and that helped the exploration stand out for me. That is my response to the art direction, rather than a technical graphics score.

For players who enjoy making a home but also want exploration beyond it, the planet-and-Starsea setup is a clear reason to look at Petit Planet. It does not mean infinite worlds or unlimited content; it means there is an appealing contrast between home and the places beyond it.

So what makes Petit Planet worth a look for farming-and-social players? For me, it is the way the interests line up: farming and everyday activities on a planet I can make my own, character friendships through Neighbors, other players at the Galactic Bazaar, and the Starsea to explore beyond home.

I found the mechanics approachable, and the experience felt relaxing in a way that suited the kind of daily life-sim play I enjoy. That is a personal impression from the beta, not a promise that everyone will react the same way.

This is the positive case I would make for Petit Planet. It offers a distinct galactic setting for familiar cozy interests, and that combination worked for me. If those are the things you want from a farming-and-social game, it is a strong reason to keep it in mind for December.

There are still a few limits to this recommendation. Petit Planet has a stamina mechanism. The fact that I found the game friendly and relaxing does not mean every action is unrestricted, or that the game has no stamina, tasks or other limits.

The planned business model is free-to-play with optional in-app purchases. Final items, rates and prices are subject to the information in the game after official launch. My beta impressions are not enough evidence to call the final game cosmetic-only or free of pay-to-win concerns.

And I cannot turn one test experience into a verdict on years of updates or replay value. Those things need the released game and more time. These are questions the available information does not settle, and I would keep them separate from the parts of the beta I enjoyed.

None of that takes away from what I enjoyed: making my planet feel like home, the everyday activities, the Neighbors and the sense of discovery beyond my planet.

My recommendation is to put Petit Planet on your December watchlist if farming, personal-space decorating and social features are your kind of life sim. The beta gave me concrete reasons to be interested: a planet that felt like home, enjoyable everyday activities, warm character encounters and exploration with that romantic, starry feel.

The galactic setting is the extra reason I would point it out. These familiar interests have their own setting and identity here, and that was a big part of the appeal for me.
